How it works

The road has to hold the axle at a fixed height, so wherever the wheel touches down, the ground must sit exactly as far below the axle as the wheel's edge is from its centre. Rolling without sliding fixes the rest. In symbols: y = −r and dx = r dθ, so x(θ) = ∫r dθ. Backwards, θ(x) = ∫dx / (−y).

What your shape decides

Your outline and where you put the axle decide the whole road: its shape, how long one length of it is, how high the axle rides, and whether a road exists at all. You choose the speed, the tilt, the spacing, and the cut size. Nothing else is adjustable, because nothing else should be.

Checked, not claimed

A square gives a row of hanging-chain curves, exactly −a cosh(x/a), to better than one part in a million. A circle gives a flat line. Both run as tests on every change, along with the fact that the road is exactly as long as the wheel's edge. Tilting the road is exact too: nothing in the derivation ever mentioned gravity.
